[p001] (body) Newton’s Tree submits these comments in response to CDRH’s discussion paper on GenAI-enabled medical devices. Our comments come from our work to evaluate, deploy, govern, and monitor clinical AI in healthcare systems. We recommend three separate assurance layers: competency testing, clinical confirmation, and operational assurance. We also include anonymized examples from real deployments. These examples show why benchmark results alone cannot give sufficient assurance of safety and effectiveness.

[p012] (att1 p.2) Risk assessment — Question 1: The two-axis framework — The two-axis framework is not sufficient for risk assessment.
[p013] (att1 p.2) Device activity does not equal the probability of harm. A highly automated device can have narrow limits and strong controls. An advisory device can cause harm through automation bias.
[p014] (att1 p.2) CDRH should continue to use the standard concepts of probability and severity.
[p015] (att1 p.2) The risk analysis should include: The unsafe device behavior.
[p016] (att1 p.2) The possible harm.
[p017] (att1 p.2) The probability of the harm.
[p018] (att1 p.2) The available risk controls.
[p019] (att1 p.2) The remaining risk after the controls operate.
[p020] (att1 p.2) The analysis must include more than an incorrect output. A GenAI device can also fail through omission, harmful agreement, delayed escalation, or an unauthorized action.
[p021] (att1 p.2) Reversibility, time pressure, traceability, and available safeguards should modify the risk analysis. CDRH does not need more graphical axes.
[p022] (att1 p.2) Question 2: General and patient-specific output — CDRH should use one clear division:
[p023] (att1 p.2) General output.
[p024] (att1 p.2) Patient-specific output.
[p025] (att1 p.2) General output does not use information about one patient.
[p026] (att1 p.2) Patient-specific output uses or applies to one patient’s symptoms, history, images, measurements, records, or conversation.
[p027] (att1 p.2) CDRH should not classify risk by wording, tone, specificity, or degree of recommendation.
[p028] (att1 p.2) These features are difficult to define. Manufacturers can also change the wording without changing the function.
[p029] (att1 p.2) CDRH should treat patient-specific output as potentially action-directing. The possible consequence of the output should then determine the risk category.
[p030] (att1 p.3) A statement that says “consider increasing the dose” can have the same effect as a direct instruction. A warning or disclaimer does not remove this risk.
[p031] (att1 p.3) Healthcare professional review can reduce the remaining risk. However, the manufacturer must prove that this control is effective. The manufacturer must include automation bias in this evaluation.
[p032] (att1 p.3) Question 3: Patient-facing devices — A patient-facing device can have more risk when the patient cannot independently assess the output.
[p033] (att1 p.3) However, CDRH should not assume that patients lack useful knowledge or capability. A well-designed device can improve access and patient control.

[p281] (att1 p.16) Conclusion — CDRH should use three assurance layers.
[p282] (att1 p.16) Competency must show that the final device can perform its task and remain within scope.
[p283] (att1 p.16) Clinical confirmation must show that the device works in the intended clinical setting.
[p284] (att1 p.16) Operational assurance must show that the device continues to work after deployment.
[p285] (att1 p.16) CDRH should use a clear division between general output and patient-specific output. It should not regulate small differences in wording.
[p286] (att1 p.16) Manufacturers should use deterministic controls for boundaries that a system can measure.
[p287] (att1 p.16) Postmarket monitoring must connect each signal to an accountable action. The available actions must include investigation, restriction, pause, rollback, and withdrawal.
