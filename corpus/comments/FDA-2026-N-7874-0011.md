[p001] (body) Generative AI-enabled Medical Device are hugely problematic. They can have systemic bias stemming from non-representative data. For example the data used to configure their algorithms could be source from flawed studies where the data sets weren't broad enough to consider all the complications that can arise in the health of human. These data sets could be flawed by understudying race, age, or other demographics that weren't taken into account, or were sourced from a study that had a narrow or small study group.
[p002] (body) There is also the question of whether generative AI-enabled medical devices present any unresolved legal liabilities. There could be IP-theft in their designs or they could be so improperly designed that they lack sufficient privacy protections which don't meet HIPAA standards. Their use could open the government up to huge lawsuits and liabilities. Also who would be held accountable were there any of these risks? Would it be the AI manufacturer or the US Government? This needs thorough congressional oversight before going public.
[p003] (body) Finally, the “black box” nature of complex models where the actually workings of the AI remain unknown to most of the developers and users presents an issue where, if there were data breeches or inaccurate information used in health practices which results in harm, there currently is no-one who stands to be accountable for such things. The AI manufacturers need to be held financially and legally accountable for ALL screw ups, data issues, or harm that these devices may do.
[p004] (body) Until those questions are resolved and settled, Generative AI-enabled Medical Device shouldn't be allowed into use.
