[p001] (body) I. Statement of Support — This comment strongly supports CDRH’s proactive, lifecycle-based regulatory framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI)-enabled medical devices.
[p002] (body) Artificial intelligence in healthcare is the defining frontier of modern clinical medicine. When paired with robust oversight and safeguards, GenAI has the transformative potential to save millions of lives annually, alleviate severe clinician burnout, eradicate diagnostic blind spots, and democratize clinical access.
[p003] (body) We strongly support CDRH’s competency-based evaluation model (integrating standardized benchmarking with risk-tiered clinical confirmation) and urge the Agency to ensure clear, streamlined pathways for high-impact use cases:
[p004] (body) Radiology Second-Reads: Double-checking imaging scans alongside radiologists to catch missed malignancies, subtle fractures, and early-stage disease before final sign-off.
[p005] (body) Primary Care Ambient Scribing: Generating structured clinical notes during consultations so physicians can eliminate clerical burdens and focus entirely on face-to-face patient care.
[p006] (body) Longitudinal EHR Surveillance: Analyzing electronic health records to detect hidden patterns, deteriorating trends, and unflagged diagnoses before they become acute emergencies.
[p007] (body) Blood Laboratory & IVD Analysis: Evaluating multi-analyte blood lab panels to identify systemic shifts, acute organ damage, and early sepsis that static alerts miss.
[p008] (body) II. Implementation Recommendations — Two-Axis Risk Calibration (Questions 1–6): Anchor ambient scribing, EHR screening, and HCP-supervised imaging second-reads firmly in lower-risk categories, reserving high-intensity premarket scrutiny for unmonitored autonomous execution.
[p009] (body) Competency Benchmarking (Questions 7–10): Prioritize Safety Escalation (S.1) and Quantitative Analysis (E.3) to guarantee numerical precision and prevent confabulations in blood lab analysis.
[p010] (body) Voluntary Master Files (Question 25): Fully operationalize Foundation Model Device Master Files (MAFs) so downstream developers can leverage upstream model cards without exposing proprietary weights.
[p011] (body) Postmarket Monitoring (Questions 18–24): Enable machine-based supervisory agents and flexible Predetermined Change Control Plans (PCCPs) under FD&C Act § 515C to govern model drift without requiring burdensome supplemental clearances.
[p012] (body) III. Requested Action — FDA should advance this discussion paper into formal Draft Guidance, establishing streamlined clearance pathways for HCP-supervised AI tools while operationalizing the Foundation Model MAF program to accelerate safe, life-saving healthcare innovation.
